What Are Axolotls?

Axolotls (Ambystoma mexicanum) are extraordinary aquatic salamanders that have captured the imagination of scientists and pet enthusiasts worldwide. Often called "Mexican walking fish," they are actually amphibians, not fish at all.

Neoteny: Forever Young

What makes axolotls truly unique is their neotenic nature. Unlike most amphibians that undergo metamorphosis, axolotls retain their larval features throughout their entire lives. This means they keep their distinctive feathery external gills, aquatic lifestyle, and tadpole-like dorsal fin even as adults.

Native Habitat

Axolotls are endemic to the lake complex of Xochimilco near Mexico City. Sadly, they are critically endangered in the wild due to urbanization, water pollution, and invasive species.

Physical Characteristics

Adult axolotls typically reach 9-12 inches in length and can live 10-15 years in captivity. They have wide heads, lidless eyes, and that characteristic smile.

The Albino Trait

The albino trait in axolotls is one of the most visually striking color variations, characterized by a complete lack of melanin pigmentation.

Genetic Basis

Albinism in axolotls is caused by a recessive genetic mutation that prevents the production of melanin.

Physical Appearance

Albino axolotls have pure white or pale golden skin with bright red or pink eyes.

Axolotls and Cancer Research

Axolotls possess an extraordinary ability that has made them invaluable to cancer research: they can regenerate entire organs and limbs without forming tumors.

Regeneration Without Cancer

When humans undergo rapid cell division, there's a significant risk of cancerous growth. Axolotls, however, can regrow entire limbs with virtually zero cancer risk.

Where To Get An Axolotl

If you're interested in keeping an axolotl as a pet, it's crucial to obtain one responsibly.

Reputable Breeders

The best source for healthy axolotls is reputable breeders who specialize in these animals.

Legal Considerations

Check your local laws before acquiring an axolotl. They're illegal to own in some states.

How To Take Care Of An Axolotl

Axolotls require specific care to thrive.

Tank Requirements

Minimum 20-gallon long tank for one adult axolotl.

Temperature Control

This is critical. Axolotls must be kept at 60-64°F.

Feeding

Adults: Earthworms, nightcrawlers every 2-3 days.
