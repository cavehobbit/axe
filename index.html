What are Axolotls? (In-Depth)

Axolotls (Ambystoma mexicanum) are an extraordinary biological phenomenon. Native exclusively to the ancient lake complex of Xochimilco near Mexico City, they possess traits that defy normal amphibian logic.

Gods of the Aztecs

In ancient Aztec mythology, it was believed that Xolotl, the god of fire, lightning, and death - disguised himself as an axolotl to avoid being sacrificed. The name "axolotl" directly translates to "water dog" or "water monster" in the Nahuatl language.

Neoteny vs. Metamorphosis

Most amphibians (like frogs) start life with gills and metamorphose to develop lungs and live on land. Axolotls are neotenic, meaning they hit the pause button on evolution. They reach full sexual maturity around 18-24 months of age and grow up to 9-12 inches long, all while maintaining their larval appearance.. feathery gills, a dorsal fin, and an entirely aquatic lifestyle.

Common Pigmentations

In the wild, the most common pigmentation is the "Wild Type." These axolotls are beautifully camouflaged in mottled shades of olive, dark brown, or black, sprinkled with shiny golden flecks called iridophores.

Unmatched Regeneration

An axolotl can completely regenerate a lost limb in just a few weeks. Amazingly, they can regenerate the exact same limb perfectly up to 5 separate times! After 5 times, the limb may grow back slightly deformed or stop regenerating. They can also seamlessly regenerate up to half of their heart, massive chunks of their brain, and their spinal cord.

Axolotl Morphs & The Albino Trait

While wild axolotls are typically a dark, mottled brown or green, laboratory breeding has produced several incredible "morphs."

The Historic Tiger Salamander Experiment

True albinism did not originate naturally in the wild axolotl population. In the late 1960s, a prominent American embryologist named Dr. Rufus R. Humphrey, working out of Indiana University in the USA, needed an albino axolotl for genetic tracking.

He successfully bred a mutant albino Tiger Salamander (a close genetic cousin) with a wild axolotl. After generations of careful breeding, he isolated the albino gene while restoring the axolotl's neotenic (water-bound) traits. This resulted in the pale-skinned, red-eyed albino axolotls we see today.

Does this cause Morphing?

Because Tiger Salamanders naturally metamorphose and walk on land, introducing their DNA into the captive axolotl gene pool accidentally increased the likelihood of spontaneous metamorphosis in some captive lines. Today, highly stressed captive axolotls occasionally (though rarely) morph due to these ancient hybrid genetics.

Golden Axolotls

Golden axolotls (Golden Albinos) lack dark melanin but retain yellow pigments (xanthophores) and reflective cells (iridophores), resulting in a brilliant, glowing golden appearance with clear eyes.

Correlation Between Axolotls & Cancer

The axolotl holds the key to the future of oncology (cancer research) and regenerative medicine.

Scarring vs. Blastema

When mammals suffer a wound, the body rushes to patch it with non-functional, fibrotic scar tissue. When an axolotl loses a limb, it doesn't scar. Instead, local cells "dedifferentiate" (revert to a stem-cell-like state) to form a mass called a Blastema.

Positional Memory

How do the cells know what to grow? Connective tissue cells in the axolotl retain "positional memory." They contain a biological GPS blueprint (the X/Y/Z axis) of exactly where they are located, so the blastema knows to grow a wrist and fingers, rather than just an elbow.

The mTOR Pathway and Tumor Suppression

In humans, the rapid cell division required to regrow a limb would almost certainly mutate into cancer. Axolotls regulate this growth using the mTOR pathway. This biological switch controls cell survival, growth, and proliferation. Axolotl DNA heavily polices this pathway, allowing explosive growth to rebuild the limb, and instantly shutting it down the millisecond the limb is finished - completely avoiding tumors.

Where to get an Axolotl

Deciding to purchase an axolotl is a 10 to 15-year commitment. Finding a responsible source is vital to ensuring a healthy pet.

USA and Canada Communities

Axolotls are widely bred and much easier to find in the United States and Canada. There are massive networks of ethical hobbyist breeders who carefully track genetics to avoid severe inbreeding. Note: They are illegal to import across state lines into California, Maine, New Jersey, and D.C. due to invasive species laws.

Worldwide Shipping & CITES

Because axolotls are an endangered species, international shipping requires highly regulated CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species) permits. This makes shipping them overseas incredibly expensive and difficult. If you live outside North America, look for local breeders in your specific country.

How to take care of an Axolotl (In-Depth)
WARNING: These guidelines are a general overview and may not work perfectly for every specific situation!

Water Chemistry: Nitrogen & pH

You cannot put an axolotl in tap water. Parameters must be exactly: Ammonia 0 ppm, Nitrite 0 ppm, and Nitrates under 20 ppm. The pH must be kept stable between 6.5 and 8.0.

Temperature & Substrate

Temperature is the #1 killer of captive axolotls. The water must remain between 60°F - 64°F (15°C - 18°C).

Forced Morphing

If an axolotl is exposed to high amounts of Iodine, highly stressful water conditions, or massive temperature spikes, it forces them to undergo metamorphosis into a terrestrial salamander. This is incredibly cruel and drastically shortens their lifespan.

Regeneration of the Axolotl Limb

Abstract Reference: PMC4895312

The Mexican axolotl (Ambystoma mexicanum) serves as a premier model for vertebrate regeneration. Upon amputation of a limb, the axolotl does not form permanent scar tissue as mammals do. Instead, the epidermis rapidly covers the wound site.


Cells beneath this wound epidermis begin to dedifferentiate, losing their mature cellular identity. They form a mass of progenitor cells known as the blastema. The formation and proliferation of the blastema are highly dependent on nerve signaling and the presence of critical immune cells, specifically macrophages.


Without macrophages, the axolotl will form scar tissue (fibrosis) and fail to regenerate, proving that immune regulation is the absolute key to unlocking scar-free healing. Once the blastema reaches a critical mass, the cells redifferentiate into bone, muscle, blood vessels, and skin, perfectly replacing the lost appendage.

Key Definitions

Neoteny: The retention of juvenile or larval traits in the adult form. Axolotls remain in their aquatic, gilled stage their whole lives.

Blastema: A mass of undifferentiated cells capable of growth and regeneration into organs or body parts.

Macrophages: A type of white blood cell that engulfs and digests cellular debris. They are the "switch" that prevents scarring in axolotls.

The Main Idea: Helping Mammals

When mammals (like humans) suffer a severe injury or heart attack, the body prioritizes closing the wound quickly by creating fibrous scar tissue. Scar tissue patches the hole but ruins the organ's function.

Axolotls bypass the scarring phase. If researchers can figure out how to chemically mimic the axolotl's macrophage response in humans, we could potentially regenerate damaged human hearts, repair spinal cords, and regrow lost tissues without debilitating scars.

Ongoing Research

In 2018, scientists fully sequenced the axolotl genome. It has 32 billion base pairs - 10 times larger than the human genome!

Current research at institutions like Harvard is focused on mapping the exact RNA sequences that activate the blastema. By comparing this massive genetic code to humans, they are finding the exact genetic "switches" we are missing.
