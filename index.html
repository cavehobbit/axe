Featured Axolotl Fact

Axolotls are sometimes called "Mexican walking fish," but they're actually salamanders that never leave the water! They keep their juvenile features throughout their entire lives, including those adorable feathery gills.

Quick Care Tip

Water Temperature: Keep your axolotl's tank between 60-64°F (15-18°C). They prefer cooler water than most aquarium animals!

Axolotl Care Guide

So you want to keep an axolotl? These amazing creatures make wonderful pets, but they do have some specific needs. Here's everything you need to know to keep your axie happy and healthy!

Tank Setup

Size Matters! Your axolotl needs plenty of space to swim around. A 20-gallon long tank is the absolute minimum for one adult axolotl, but bigger is always better. If you want to keep multiple axolotls, add 10 gallons for each additional axie.

Substrate: This is super important! Axolotls are notorious for eating anything that fits in their mouths. Use either fine sand (they can pass this through their system) or no substrate at all. Never use gravel or small stones - these can cause fatal blockages!

Water Conditions

Temperature: 60-64°F (15-18°C) - Use a chiller if needed!

pH: 6.5-7.5

Ammonia: 0 ppm

Nitrites: 0 ppm

Nitrates: Under 20 ppm

Warning: Axolotls are extremely sensitive to poor water quality. Test your water regularly and do 20% water changes weekly!

Feeding

Axolotls are carnivores and love meaty foods! Here are the best options:

Juveniles (daily feeding): Bloodworms, blackworms, brine shrimp

Adults (every 2-3 days): Earthworms, nightcrawlers, axolotl pellets, frozen bloodworms

Treats: Frozen shrimp, small pieces of fish

Pro tip: Earthworms from the pet store are the perfect staple food for adult axolotls!

Tank Decorations

Keep it simple and safe! Live or silk plants work great. Avoid anything with sharp edges or small parts. Some good options include: Java ferns, anubias, moss balls, caves, driftwood (make sure it's aquarium-safe)

Remember: Axolotls can live 10-15 years with proper care. They're a long-term commitment, but totally worth it!

Amazing Axolotl Facts!

Prepare to have your mind blown! Axolotls are some of the most incredible animals on Earth. Here are some facts that will make you appreciate these amazing creatures even more:

The Regeneration Masters

This is the big one! Axolotls can regenerate almost any part of their body, including entire limbs, parts of their heart, brain tissue, and even portions of their spinal cord. The regenerated limb is identical to the original - same bones, muscles, nerves, everything!

Even more amazing: they can regenerate the same limb multiple times throughout their lives. Scientists are studying axolotls hoping to unlock the secrets of regeneration for human medicine.

Forever Young

Axolotls exhibit something called "neoteny" - they keep their juvenile characteristics throughout their entire adult lives. While most salamanders lose their gills and develop lungs to live on land, axolotls stay aquatic forever. They're basically eternal teenagers!

Ancient Aztec Legends

The name "axolotl" comes from the Aztec language Nahuatl, meaning "water dog" or "water monster." According to Aztec mythology, axolotls are the earthly form of the god Xolotl, who was the god of lightning and death.

Rainbow of Colors

Wild axolotls are typically dark brown or black with golden speckles, but captive breeding has produced amazing color variations including leucistic (pink with dark eyes), albino (white with red eyes), golden, copper, and melanoid (solid black).

Science Superstars

Axolotls have been used in scientific research for over 150 years! They've helped scientists understand development, genetics, and regeneration. Many major biological discoveries have been made using axolotls as research subjects.

Critically Endangered

Sadly, wild axolotls are critically endangered. They only exist naturally in the lake system of Xochimilco near Mexico City, and habitat destruction and pollution have devastated their population. There may be fewer than 1,000 left in the wild.

Breathing Superpowers

Axolotls have three ways to breathe! They can absorb oxygen through their skin, use their feathery external gills, and they also have rudimentary lungs that they can use to gulp air from the surface.

Smart Cookies

Don't let their simple appearance fool you - axolotls are quite intelligent! They can recognize their owners, learn feeding schedules, and even be trained to respond to different colored lights. Some axolotl keepers report that their pets have distinct personalities.
